Final Objective: A feature-rich engine to build Fire Emblem Style Strategy RPGs with
with a focus in emerging ludonarratives

Main inspirations:  
FE4 and 5 FE in ludonarrative resonance, gameplay flow, pacing
FE5 in game balance and jankyness 
GBA FE in presentation, UI, game feel and approachability 
FE9 and 10 in the base mechanics and stuff
3DS FE (mostly awakening and fates) in UI, QoL and UX, as well as unit customization freedom
FE2 / Echoes in experimental mechanics

Priorities (2026-10-10)
Ordered by value to the ludonarrative goal, what each item unlocks, and whether it gets
more expensive the longer it waits.

Tier 1 - do soon, the cost keeps growing
 1. (X) Other factions - units are PLAYER/ENEMY with ~20 hardcoded checks; every new feature
    adds more. Core to FE4/5-style emergent story (allies, neutrals, three-way fights,
    recruitment). Berserk can come along with it.
 2. (X) Language groundwork - no tr() yet. Just route player-facing strings through tr() and
    give event text keys; full translation can wait. Gets worse with every line of dialogue.
 3. (X) Data out of .gd constants - into resources/JSON. Needed before the database editor,
    multiple campaigns and per-campaign feature toggles.

Tier 2 - turns it into a real campaign
 4. (X) Currency, shops and services - needed to pace a campaign; forging/enchanting/repair
    costs depend on it.
 5. (X) Finish the race pass - the decided items in docs/races.md (stat caps, growths,
    movement priority, Fae regen, Ent class limits).
 6. Permadeath mechanics - very ludonarrative, mostly self-contained.

Tier 3 - content, once the systems settle
 Mys, janky spell effects, weapon traits then forging/enchanting,
 transforming, monster classes, mounted equivalents, biography gameplay effects.

Tier 4 - the milestone
 Start the 10 chapter proof of concept after Tier 1 + items 4-5, and let it reorder the rest.

Later - presentation and tooling (cheap exception: class icons for placeholders,
makes playtesting easier to read)
